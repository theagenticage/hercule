The InboxHi-fi design, session B Open the window

A card index for everything out there.

This is where every outside thing that concerns you lands, before you take it on. It never scrolls. A pull request looks like a pull request, a ticket like a ticket, and you can tell them apart by logo alone. Signals live inside their card, so the feed becomes things with a history.

One rule: a stack shows the oldest few, and the rest wait in a pile.

The lo-fi Inbox was right about cards and had two weaknesses: still a long page, and it repeated itself. Both are gone.

Things rest in a few stacks that stand in fixed places. The first card stands whole, because it has waited longest. A few strips lie behind it, and the rest wait in a pile with a count on it. Clear a card and the next rises out of the pile. Nothing scrolls, and nothing changes place by itself. You learn where things are, the way you know your own desk, and there is always desk to see.

This one rule is why the page looks the same at 34 cards and at 300: more volume makes a pile thicker and a number larger, and nothing else. There is no compact mode to switch to.

The page says only what differs.

The lo-fi card said Review requested on every card, and showed a green check on every card. That is the repetition. So the rule here is: what is usual says nothing.

  • The stack's heading says why most cards are in it, so a card in To review shows only who asks: Juno Park.
  • What is unusual gets the words: Felix asked again, Checks failed, Omar requested changes. Colour stays off the sentence and on one small shape, as it does in Linear, so red means something.
  • A healthy pull request shows no status at rest, and on the shelf a ticket is one line. In a run of the same logo the repeats step back, so the eye finds where provenance changes.
  1. 1Mark. The plugin's logo, 20px, the first thing in the row. Marks line up down the stack, so provenance reads as one straight line.
  2. 2Title. 13/18 medium on a strip, shared with Work, and 15/21 on the card that leads.
  3. 3Pace. How long it has waited, measured against the patience of its kind. It darkens as patience runs out.
  4. 4Reason. The latest signal, in one sentence, with the person in it.
  5. 5The rest. A quiet count of the signals behind it.
  6. 6Status. One shape from a fixed set, plus a tone. Only here does colour speak.
  7. 7Identifier and context. Shown on a whole card, and on approach.
  8. 8Facts. At most three short ones, in a fixed set of forms.

One card, drawn from a table. A plugin never ships UI.

Every card below comes from the same function, which has no branch on a kind. All a kind can do is fill in a table: its mark and hue, its stock, its density, its pace, its identifier, its status shapes, its facts. Quayside is a plugin I made up. It ships this table and one mark, and no code.

So GitHub reads as GitHub (#6213, a diff in green and red, a fork shape), Linear reads as Linear (tight, a ledger stock, a key like SHOP-360, progress circles, priority bars), and a mail thread reads as a letter on manila, with no status at all.

Signals live inside their entity.

At rest a card shows the latest reason it is here, and a quiet count of the rest. A second review request, a mention or a failed check adds a line to the same card. It never adds a card, and it never moves one.

That moment gets the one flourish the Inbox allows itself: the card takes a small knock, the old sentence rolls away, the new one rolls in, the count ticks, and a rim of light fades in the colour of the news. Then everything is still again.

Different kinds keep different time.

The working names are gone. Rather than sort by speed, the page says what you would do next.

Handing off should feel like giving it to a capable colleague.

Hover a card and its actions take the place of the wait: the workflow that fits, by name, then Take, then a close. With one workflow it is one click. With several, a small menu names them. Press H, or pick the card up and drop it on a colleague.

Then it plays as one chain, and the cause always comes first. The wait gives way and the colleague comes up into its place, at work, while the card still stands. Only then does the card give up its weight: it sinks a little, lies flat, the sentence rolls to Agent review has it, and the card's own mark lifts off and flies to Overview, whose icon dips to receive it. Undo plays the whole thing backwards. Take it on yourself and your own monogram stands there instead.

The card stays where it is while you are here. You watch the stack go quiet from the top, the way ticked items stay put in Things. When nothing in view waits any more, the stack turns over: the held cards settle to the foot line, counted as on Work with a way through, and the pile deals the next few.

A card opens where it lies.

Its corner stays put and it unfolds across its neighbours, so the eye does not travel and nothing behind it moves. The title and the logo grow from where they lay. Open, it shows the signals in order, its facts, and its moves as the ledger Intake already uses. Esc folds it back, from wherever it is: closing is the opening played backwards.

Press J and the sheet stays put while the next card takes its place, so you can read down a stack without your eyes moving.

One cursor, and a rhythm.

The pointer and the keyboard share one current card, and every move moves on to the next card that still waits. H, T and E work through a stack with one hand.

Dismiss works like closing a browser tab: the next card rises into the place, so the same click at the same spot dismisses the next. Undo sits in the foot of the column you are working in, covers no card, and goes as far back as you like, from anywhere. A hand-off's Undo says plainly that it stops the run.

Nothing else moves under your hand. A new card that arrives while you are in its stack waits at the heading, 1 new, and joins when you leave.

Intake stays the noisy layer. Things rise out of it.

Intake moves behind the Inbox, the way Office sits behind Overview: one pill, Cards and Intake, and one sidebar row fewer. It is now grouped by entity, so an error with 200 events is one line. This is a sketch, as asked.

Something rises in two ways. Hercule raises it, and it arrives in Noticed. Or you press Raise to Inbox, the row lifts, and a token flies to Cards.

Where it lives, and how it asks for you.

Inbox takes the row Intake had. It tells you it needs you without nagging:

  • The row shows a plain grey number, not a marigold badge: the cards on the clock, the sum of the counts you see on the page.
  • A signal landing on a card you already have never raises the number. Only a new thing does, and the digit rolls once. No bounce, no dock badge.

Every state is designed.

The Inbox at zero is a small sunrise: Hercule's head comes up over a brass horizon, and the rays draw themselves out from it when the last card leaves. First run is the page itself, not a dialog: each stack's first card asks to be connected. Loading uses the real cards with their words held back, so nothing shifts when they arrive. Nothing fails silently: offline and a failing source each say what they mean for what you see.

What I rejected, and what is new.

I sketched three compositions on the real tokens. A grid of cards with a shelf beside it was a long wall. A desk of piles was wonderfully calm and hid too much. The card index read as quiet as the thread list you still prefer. Critics then showed it was still a wall of text, and the piles turned out to be the answer: the index on top, the pile under it, one object.

The system gains: stacks and their one rule, the plate, stock with a trace of hue, marks in a declared hue, patience as a field of a kind, the peek, the drop and the rail. The full list is in DESIGN.md, the motion in MOTION.md.
