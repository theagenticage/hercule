Intake, rethought

How does Intake turn 212 signals into shipped work while costing you the least attention?

Agents are cheap and come in swarms. What runs out first is your attention, and your ability to make the calls that matter, fast. Today's Intake shows what came in, and shows it well. It does not get work moving. Below are three answers. Each one pulls a different lever, and each is drawn in Crew Bureau 2 for the desktop, the web and the phone, on the same morning: Tuesday 29 September 2026, 09:41.

1DeckSpeed per decision. One complete decision at a time, answered with one key or one swipe. 2The LineVisible consequence. Intake is the head of a production line, and every answer moves work down it. 3Standing OrdersFewer decisions. Any answer can become a standing order, so Intake shrinks to the exceptions.

Kept from the spec

  • Proposing is not doing. Triage never starts, merges or replies. Your click authorises the operation, and anything that acts without a click is a start trigger you wrote, with a Spawn Bound.
  • Since you last checked. The page is framed on the marker (yesterday 18:20), pinned in the URL, and the receipt adds up to 212.
  • Needs a call is verdict-based, never priority-based.
  • Made from is on every proposal. Detail opens in a drawer or a sheet, never a page.
  • Retention is stated: events are kept 90 days, and no list is cut short in silence.

  • "Work on task" as a shipped workflow, and a human gate inside a run.
  • One spine for Intake and Check-in, still an open question.
  • Presence routing, webhook sources, finer muting, an archive, and more than one user.
Direction 1 · the lever is speed per decision

Deck

Intake deals one complete decision at a time. You answer with one key or one swipe, the next card moves up, and a strip shows how many are left and about how long they take. When the last one is answered, the page says so and shows what your answers started.

Brief. Today you read a page, then hunt for the buttons. Deck turns the morning into a short task with an end: 9 decisions, about 2 minutes, 9 keys. Every answer on the card carries its describe line, so you never answer blind, and Undo holds each answer for 5 seconds. Everything that needs no answer waits on a quiet shelf at the side.

9keys clear this morning, about 2 minutes. Today each decision is a scroll, a read and a click
13keys clear 90 decisions at ten times the load: similar decisions share one card, and S splits it
6buttons in view on the desktop (today 13), every one with its describe line and its key
1 of 9decisions answerable without scrolling (today 3 of 9). The other 8 are one key away

Main elements

  • StripThe count, an estimate, and one pip per card, grouped Now, Needs a call, Today, When you can. Now is one red pip; nothing pulses.
  • Decision cardKind, topic, priority and project, the title, the gist and made from. Then the answers as a ledger: button, describe line, fine print, key.
  • KeysReturn takes the suggested answer, 1 to 9 the others. J and K move, Space opens the evidence, S splits a batch, U undoes.
  • BatchSimilar decisions on one card, every member named. "Accept all 22" or Split, which deals each member with its own describe line.
  • Moving and UndoWhat your earlier answers started, at the side. The latest answer is held for 5 seconds before it is sent.
  • Quiet shelfFYI, attached to Tasks, no action, and a new Connection still listening. Each is a fold with the marks of its systems.
  • Panel and Live Activity⌥Space deals the deck over any app on the Mac. On the phone, the next decision waits on the lock screen.

Away from the page, and in detail

The deck comes to you; the evidence opens beside the card; the end of the deck is a real state.

Why it beats The Line and Standing Orders

Both others put several things in front of you at once: The Line the whole line, Standing Orders the tiers and the orders beside them. Deck asks the least of you at the moment you decide: one card, what each answer does, one key. It is also the closest fit for where v1 reaches you away from the desk, which is chat and the phone. A card is one message and a swipe is one button, so the same decision reads the same everywhere. And apart from batches, it needs no new record and no new kind of trigger. It changes how today's decisions are dealt, not what they are.

Why it is 1000x better than today

Today a decision costs a scroll, a read and a hunt: 348 words in view, 13 buttons, and 6 of 9 decisions below the fold. Deck gives the morning an end you can see: 9 decisions, about 2 minutes, then "No decisions wait on you". At ten times the load today's page grows ten times longer; Deck deals the 90 decisions in 13 keys and about 4 minutes. And because the panel and the lock screen bring the next card to you, a decision waits for your next free minute, not for your next visit to the page.

At ten times the load

  • 90 decisions on 13 cards, about 4 minutes. The count says both.
  • Batches carry the volume: "Accept all 22", "Close all 9", "Reply to all 6". Each lists every member, and S splits it.
  • Now stays one red pip, and the burning proposal still comes first.
  • Moving shows the 4 newest runs and "60 more in Runs".

What it pulls from after v1

  • Fast lane: a proposal that arrives between batches adds a pip; the card in focus never moves under your hand.
  • Park: a fourth ledger row and a fourth thumb-zone row. Both fit.
  • Presence routing: the panel on the Mac, the lock screen on the phone, whichever you are at.
  • "Work on task": one more answer on the card.

What it contradicts

  • Pinned semantics, "lead card and condensed rows": only one card has detail, so 1 of 9 is answerable without scrolling.
  • Spec 10 §7.4 for batches: "Accept all 22" is 22 operations, and its describe line is written by the client, not the core. Who writes it, and how a partial failure shows, must be decided. In chat, triage must mark the group before delivery, or the batch is 22 messages.
  • Spec 17, one window: the panel is a second window, about 18 MB while open. The alternative keeps one window: ⌥Space raises the main window in a compact deck. The menu bar item is not in spec 17 either.
  • Spec 10 §2.4 marks Accept as the main answer. Deck makes triage's suggestion the main answer.

How to adopt it

  1. Ship the card's answer ledger and the keys (↩, 1 to 9, J, K, U) in today's Intake. No spec change.
  2. Add the strip and the clear state.
  3. Add the phone deck and the chat message (spec 12 §11.6).
  4. Decide batches with triage marking the groups. Measure the panel against spec 17 last.

Direction 2 · the lever is visible consequence

The Line

Intake is the head of a production line. Four stages read from left to right: what came in, your call, what is moving, and what shipped. Every answer visibly moves work one stage down, and every shipped thing traces back to the events it was made from.

Brief. Today an answer disappears into silence. The Line keeps it in view. You see 212 events become 9 calls, 4 runs and 3 shipped things on one screen, and you see how long each step took. That turns attention into something you can measure: on #1287, your call took 3h 21m of a 4h 50m lead time.

4stages on one screen, from the first event to the merged pull request (today: 1)
69%of the lead time of #1287 was your call. Today nothing on the page shows it
9buttons in view on the desktop (today 13), every one with its describe line
2 of 10decisions answerable without scrolling (today 3 of 9). The Line spends that room on consequence

Main elements

  • Flow meterFour counts with arrows between them: 212 in, 9 for you, 4 moving, 3 shipped. Each count opens its stage.
  • TapsOne tap per Connection, with its count. A new Connection shows a dashed pipe and "listening". A tripped breaker is a closed valve with the paused mark.
  • Your callTiers Now, Back from the line, Needs a call, Today, When you can. Every answer sits in place, as a ledger row: button, describe line, fine print.
  • Back from the lineA running session that asks you something comes back up the line: "Publish payments-api 2.15.0 to npm?" sits beside the proposals, not on another page.
  • MovingThe runs your earlier answers started, with their faces, workflow and time. On the desktop you can drop a decision on a workflow to start it.
  • Shipped and its traceWhat reached the world, each with its sources and lead time. The trace splits the lead time into came in, your call, moving, and review.

At ten times the load, and in detail

2,120 events, 90 calls. The line splits into topic lanes; the trace opens in a drawer or a sheet.

Why it beats Deck and Standing Orders

Deck makes each decision faster, but you still answer into silence, and fast answers with no feedback drift into rubber stamping. Standing Orders removes decisions, but you can only hand a choice to a trigger once you trust what that choice produces. The Line is where that trust comes from: it shows what every answer started and what it shipped. It is also the only direction that answers the open question of one spine for Intake and Check-in.

Why it is 1000x better than today

Today the page ends where the work begins. There are 0 places that show what an answer did. The Line closes that loop for every item: 212 events, 9 calls, 4 runs, 3 shipped, each traceable to its sources. The multiplier is not in clicks. It is in learning: once you see that your call was 69% of a lead time, you answer the Now tier first, you turn your repeat answers into standing orders, and you stop opening things that never ship.

At ten times the load

  • The shape stays; the grain changes. Each topic becomes a lane under the same four columns.
  • A lane's calls become counts by tier at fixed places, so the eye finds "now" in the same spot in every lane.
  • Moving shows stacked faces with "+11"; Shipped shows a median, not a list.
  • The one burning decision stays a full card above the lanes on the phone.

What it pulls from after v1

  • One spine for Intake and Check-in: The Line is that spine.
  • Fast lane: a tap shows a dot as an event arrives, at most one per tap every 2 seconds.
  • "Work on task" and human gates inside a run: they arrive as Back from the line.
  • Presence routing: the run you started follows you to the phone as a Live Activity.

What it contradicts

  • Separate views. It merges Intake and Check-in (pinned semantics, spec 10 §Post-v1). Approvals enter Intake, and the two since markers become one.
  • Spec 10 §8: "Intake reads Tasks, Notifications and Events, never run outputs". Moving, Shipped and the trace read runs and pull requests. No record holds a trace today.
  • It shows only the 4 runs that came through Intake. The other 12 live sessions still need a home.
  • Came in sits first, on the left, not at the foot of the page.

How to adopt it

  1. Ship the answer ledger and the taps in today's Intake. Neither departs from the spec.
  2. Add Shipped with its trace: a new controller read that joins Events, the Task, the answer, the run and the pull request.
  3. Add Moving: the runs a Task's answers started.
  4. Write the ADR for one spine, then move approvals in.

Direction 3 · the lever is fewer decisions

Standing Orders

Any answer can become a standing order: a start trigger you write, with a bound. The next time the same kind of thing comes in, it is done for you and reported afterwards. Intake shrinks to the exceptions, and beside them you see what each order did while you were away.

Brief. Today every decision reaches you every time, and the page has no way to say "and do that again next time". Standing Orders adds that sentence. "Always do this" shows you what the order would have matched last week before you make it, and every order after that shows its runs, its bound, Review, and Undo where the work can be undone. The decisions you give again and again stop reaching you.

7decisions reach you at ten times the load. In the other two directions, 90 still do
38things handled for you this week, against 9 answered by you. The lever is on the page
5 of 10decisions answerable without scrolling on the desktop (today 3 of 9), each answer with its short describe line
31past events previewed before an order is made, with the 6 it should not have matched flagged

Main elements

  • TiersNow, Needs a call, Waiting on you, Today, When you can, then Also. The tier sits in a gutter, so every row's answers line up in the same three columns.
  • Short describe lineUnder each answer on a row: the core's full line minus the names the row already shows. "Removes label proposed".
  • Lead cardThe one card: full describe lines with fine print, and "Always do this..." in its foot.
  • LeverHandled for you against answered by you, this week, as two numbers and a split bar.
  • OrderWhat it did since the marker, one line per run. Its Spawn Bound as a meter, Review, and Undo where the work can be undone.
  • Always do thisThe editor: the event in words, the workflow it starts, the bound, and a preview of last week's matches with the unwanted ones flagged.
  • Starter ordersA new Connection comes with suggested orders. None is active until you make it.

Making an order, and at ten times the load

Why it beats Deck and The Line

Deck makes each answer faster and The Line makes each answer visible, but in both you still give every answer yourself. At ten times the load they still hold 90 decisions. Standing Orders is the only direction where the number of decisions falls as the load grows, because the answers you repeat stop reaching you. And the handover stays explicit: every order is a start trigger you wrote, with a bound you chose and a preview you saw. Nothing acts on a guess.

Why it is 1000x better than today

Today the ninth dependency bump costs you the same click as the first, and the page cannot learn from your answers. Standing Orders turns a repeated answer into a trigger in one step, after it shows proof: 31 matches from last week, 6 of them flagged. Then the lever shows the return, 38 handled for you against 9 by you this week, and 1,186 against 41 at ten times the load. The multiplier is not in answering faster. It is in answering once.

At ten times the load

  • 7 decisions, not 90. Add to backlog takes the low-priority proposals; Merge dependency bumps takes the patches; Draft reply answers Intercom.
  • The orders list grows instead: 6 orders, then "9 more orders · 466 runs, none paused".
  • "Handled 57" says which order took which proposals.
  • A plugin action that changed shows "Cannot be taken" in place; the other answers still work.

What it pulls from after v1

  • Feedback that teaches triage: "Stop proposing PostHog dips under 5%" sits under Also, marked later.
  • Fast lane: orders act between batches, and the lever counts as they do.
  • Park: a fourth answer column on a row, a fourth line on the lead card.

What it contradicts

  • CONTEXT.md: "standing order" is not a word there. It is a start Trigger with a Spawn Bound, as ADR 0022 asks. Either CONTEXT.md gets the word, or the UI says "start trigger".
  • Spec 10 §2.5: an order acts without a click, which is its point. An order made from a Start answer listens on task.created, which §2.4 rules out for work workflows.
  • Spec 10 §7.7 needs a line: a Proposal resolves when its Task stops being proposed, whoever changed it.
  • Spec 10 §7.4 and spec 12 §11.6: rows and chat buttons show a short describe line, derived by a rule, not the full line.
  • Spec 10 §8: the npm approval sits in Intake under Waiting on you. Approvals belong to Check-in, and v1 keeps the two views separate.
  • Not built: Spawn Bounds, the breaker, held events, trigger.resume (#87), and plugin actions as answers. The bound meters depend on them.

How to adopt it

  1. Ship the tiers with answers in fixed columns, and propose the short describe line rule for spec 10 §7.4.
  2. Build Spawn Bounds, the breaker and held events (#87). Every order's "At most" depends on them, and today nothing enforces a bound.
  3. Offer "Always do this" on Accept and Dismiss first: one-step task.update workflows that can be undone, with the match preview and the orders list beside the tiers.
  4. Open orders to Start answers and plugin actions, then add the lever and starter orders for new Connections.

The pick

Deck, then what it lacks from the other two

Start from Deck, unless bending the number of decisions this year matters more to you than making each one fast.

Why Deck

  • It costs the least attention at the moment you decide. One card, every answer with its describe line, one key.
  • It ships in steps on today's records. The card's answer ledger and the keys need no spec change.
  • It reads the same everywhere. A card is one chat message and a swipe is one button, and in v1 the phone surface is Slack or Discord.
  • Its catch is batches. The 13 keys at ten times the load rest on them, and they are its biggest departure. Until triage marks the groups and the core writes one describe line for a batch, 90 decisions are 90 keys.

What it takes from the other two, and where it goes

  1. Now: Deck's card, ledger and keys in today's Intake.
  2. Next, from The Line: Shipped, with its lead time, under Moving in the side column. Its trace opens in the evidence drawer. This closes the gap from an answer to shipped work, and it needs the new trace read.
  3. Then, from Standing Orders, once #87 is built: "Always do this..." in the card's foot, next to Evidence. The orders join the side column under Moving, and the clear state shows the lever: 38 handled for you, 9 by you.
  4. Before the load grows: decide batches.

Pick another if

Standing Orders, if you would rather the number of decisions fall this year, and you build #87 first. It is the only direction where ten times the load is not ten times the decisions. It also has the most still to build.

The Line, if one spine for Intake and Check-in is the question to settle this year. It is the only direction that answers it, and its trace is what tells you an order is safe to make.
