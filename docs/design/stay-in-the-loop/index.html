Stay in the loop

You handed it to an agent. How do you find out how it went, and what do you do when you don't like what came back?

Intake is built to get things off your plate. Hand to an agent does that perfectly: one click, and the signal is gone. But the curiosity does not go with it. Today nothing comes back: no word when the agent lands, no result where you asked for it, no way to say "close, but not like that". Below are four answers, each a working desktop prototype on the same Tuesday morning, each pulling a different lever.

The baseline

Today: one click away, eight clicks back

The spec does this on purpose. A signal leaves To do the moment you make your move, and never waits for the work behind it (spec 10 §9.6). Hand to an agent is a move, so the signal resolves at the click with "Handed to Draft reply". What remains is a read-only run state in the Done view. A failed run goes to Check-in, which the desktop does not have in v1.

Shared by all four

The missing state: landed, but not yet seen

"The agent finished" and "I have seen it and I accept it" are two different moments. Every direction models the same lifecycle, with no new state marks: the six marks stay, and landed is the done mark plus one small marigold dot, because it waits on you.

HandedThe click. The signal leaves To do, as the spec says.
WorkingThe run is live, with the agent's own one-line notes.
Needs youA question mid-run: "Void INV-2291 in Stripe?"
LandedFinished, and you have not looked. Today this state does not exist.
SeenOpened, no verdict yet. Still yours to close.
Signed offYou accept it. Anything outward still needs your click.
Sent back with your note: attempt 2, back to working Taken over: you continue in the agent's thread Undone: the PR closed, the draft deleted Failed: the reason, the partial work, retry with a note

1Round Trip

Every handoff comes back to To do as a new signal: what you asked, beside what the agent did, with Sign off, the outward action, Send back or Take over.

Work still out waits quietly under the list in "Out with agents", and only speaks up, in marigold, when it has a Request.

Open the prototype Brief Story

Why it works

  • No new place to check. The loop closes in the list Rogier already empties every day, with the keys he already knows.
  • Closest to the spec: §9.6 stays true. The original signal still leaves at the click; a new signal asks a new move.
  • The failed run finally surfaces on the desktop, where it can be tried again with a note.

What worries me

  • Intake gets busier: results now compete with fresh signals for the same attention.
  • "Out with agents" answers Requests inside Intake, which blurs Intake and Waiting on you.
  • A fifth core kind (result), raised by the core, and a third exception to "core actions are never primary".

Try this

  • Open Marta's invoice draft, press B, type a note, ⌘↩. Watch attempt 2 ask about Stripe, then land.
  • Keep the pointer off the list for 12 seconds: Investigate lands.

"After a month, handing off stops feeling like throwing work over a wall."

2Pocket Watch

A watch next to the clock shows every handoff at a glance: ticks fill while agents work, marigold notches mark what landed, tomato marks what failed.

Each handoff carries a "Tell me" contract set at the click (when it lands, if it needs me, or the 12:30 roundup), so only what Rogier allowed interrupts him.

Open the prototype Brief Story

Why it works

  • The lowest interruption cost of the four, and the only one that respects focus by design.
  • Plays to what only the desktop has: the menu bar, notifications, a global shortcut.
  • The contract makes every interruption one Rogier asked for, and the default learns from trust.

What worries me

  • A 560px case window is thin for real review: a diff or a long draft wants the main window.
  • The riskiest native work: a colored menu-bar image, notification buttons macOS hides for unsigned builds or banner style.
  • No history and no comparison of attempts: once signed off, it is gone from view.

Try this

  • Wait 12 seconds: Investigate lands as a notification over the editor.
  • Press ⌥Space to open the watch. Try ?state=contract, then 1 2 3.

"After a month, the watch is something he reads like the time."

3Case File

Every handoff opens a case: one trail from what came in to what the world did with the result. It opens where it waits on you; scroll up to rewind.

Send back adds an attempt you can compare word by word, and a case closes only when the source shows the end: the PR merged, Marta replied.

Open the prototype Brief Story

Why it works

  • The best answer to "what if I don't like it": attempts, a pinned note, a real compare, keep either.
  • Provenance both ways: back to the mail and the Sentry events, forward to the merge. H2 links to yesterday's #1287.
  • Honest about confidence: doubts are set apart, not buried in prose.

What worries me

  • A new screen and a new record (the Case): the heaviest of the four, rated XL.
  • Mostly pull, not push: beyond a sidebar count nothing tells Rogier a case landed.
  • Matching "closed by the source" on refs the run created (#1302) is new, fiddly core work.

Try this

  • Open ?state=compare and press [ ] between attempts.
  • Open ?case=y1: yesterday's #1287, linking forward to today's regression.

"After a month, the cases read like a ledger of trust."

4The Desk

Results land as paper in an in-tray on a lacquered Art Deco desk, and Rogier signs them off when he sits down: J/K to move, ⌘↩ for the suggested answer.

Only a Request from running work rings the bell. The blotter keeps a trust ledger, and offers to let a workflow post by itself once it has earned it.

Open the prototype Brief Story

Why it works

  • Batching made physical: one sitting clears the morning, and nothing else interrupts.
  • The only direction that turns trust into fewer stops: the ledger, then an offer Rogier saves himself.
  • It is the most Crew Bureau of the four, and the one people would remember.

What worries me

  • The metaphor costs room: paper, trays and a frame leave less space for the result than a plain pane.
  • Novelty wears off; a ritual is only good if it stays fast. It clears 7 results in about a minute by keyboard.
  • It rides on the Office, which is a heavier screen than Intake, and on results drawn as paper.

Try this

  • Press S on a result and watch the stamp. Try ?state=trust.
  • Switch to ?state=floor: a colleague carries a result to Your desk.

"After a month, the bell rings twice a day and the tray is cleared three times."

One thing all four need, whichever wins: a run that was handed a signal must return a structured result (its kind, a draft or a change, the outward actions it proposes, its confidence and its doubts), and Send back must start a new attempt with a note. Three of the four builders named it their riskiest part. It is the real core work, and it is the same work for all four.

Recommendation

The pick

Build Round Trip as the spine, and borrow from the others.

Round Trip wins on the thing that matters most: it needs no new habit. Rogier already empties To do every day, with keys he knows. Making every handoff come back there closes the loop in the one place he is sure to look, and it bends the spec the least: the original signal still leaves at the click, and a new signal asks a new, honest move: look at what came back.

On its own it is not enough. It does nothing for "tell me now" and it is weak at "not like that". The other directions fix exactly those gaps. In this order:

  1. Start in the core: the structured result and the attempt with a note. Without them every direction draws a transcript in a frame.
  2. From Case File: attempts and compare, inside Round Trip's pane. Send back starts attempt 2 with the note pinned at its head; Compare shows attempt 1 and 2 side by side. Drop the separate Cases screen and record: Done's trip line already carries the history.
  3. From Pocket Watch: the "Tell me" contract at the handoff (when it lands, if it needs me, roundup), driving plain native notifications. Defer the menu-bar watch until the rest has earned it; it is the riskiest native work in the set.
  4. Later, from The Desk: the trust ledger and its offer to stop stopping, once there are 30 days of sign-offs to count. The desk itself is the best demo here, but the metaphor costs room on every result.
