Crew Bureau

Order and method: a detective bureau of small colleagues.

Hercule runs a crowd of agents. Crew Bureau gives each one a face: a small egg-headed colleague drawn from its name, whose pose is its state. Focused eyes mean working, a raised marigold hand means it waits on you, closed eyes mean asleep. Threads come first in the sidebar, as in any coding tool; the Art Deco office is one click away, for when you want to see everyone at once. Glass is a single slider, subtle by default. One green button is the suggestion; nothing glows.

Why this one

Same warmth, stricter bones.

The original Crew won on warmth and lost on basics: the office pushed threads aside, the buttons looked like candy, and the characters could have belonged to any product. Crew Bureau keeps the warmth and fixes the basics. Threads come first, as in every coding tool. One green button suggests; the rest are plain tablets. The colleagues now carry Poirot's kit: egg heads, waxed mustaches, homburgs, a monocle.

Crew Labours, the other iteration, leans on Hercules; this one leans on Poirot: order, symmetry and small details instead of heroic scale, and less glass by default, 40% against 50%.

What it trades away: a Deco face and brass ornament are more to maintain, and a detective joke wears thin if it is everywhere. So the Deco face sets only the wordmark, the logo and a few numerals; the office carries the rest.

The office

Everyone at once, in an Art Deco bureau.

The office shows the same colleagues, in the same poses, as the sidebar and the list. It adds what words cannot: where everyone is, and what the machines have room for. It is one click from Threads, not the home screen.

  • A wing per runnerEach runner is a wing with one desk per slot. A wing without an empty desk is at its cap: build-box-1, 8 of 8.
  • A desk is a sessionA lit green lamp means working. A dark lamp and a cup of tisane mean idle.
  • Your desk holds the queueA colleague that waits on you leaves a marigold note on its desk and queues at yours, with the question on its tag.
  • The case board is IntakeTriage pins each Proposal as a card and threads it to what it was made from.
  • Filing cabinets are Tasks14 open. One drawer stands open: the Tasks someone is working on.
  • Tubes carry eventsWith Event flow on, capsules travel from each Connection's brass plaque into Triage's desk.
The first run

Four steps, and the office opens around you.

The first time the desktop app opens after Hercule is installed, it finds Hercule on this Mac and sets it up in four steps: your account, a provider login, GitHub, and a first project. No browser, no terminal, no pasted address. Each finished step furnishes the room, and the run ends on your first thread. It is prototyped in three variants; the book recommends B.

Three ways to open it pick one; the rest of the chapter shows B

  • The room is the promiseBefore you type anything you see where your agents will work. Progress is furniture, not a counter.
  • Every step pays off on screenAccount: your desk, and Hercule asleep in the lobby. Providers: six free desks. GitHub: Triage and its tube. Project: your first thread.
  • No address, no token, no terminalThe app reads the address and the setup token from the Hercule Home on this Mac. You never see either.
  • Skipping is said, not hiddenA step you put off keeps its number in the ladder instead of a tick, and the last screen says where to finish it.
  • Your folder stays as it isPicking ~/Dev/webshop reads its origin remote. Threads clone from the remote into worktrees of their own.
  • Why B over AThe same room and the same cost. B shows it at full size, and the close shot of what you just added is the reward for the step.

What this design asks the spec to change

Fifteen changes, none to the domain's meaning.

  • 1Waiting on you tops both facesThe section sits above the sidebar's two faces, Threads and Hercule, instead of inside one (spec 14, App shell).
  • 2The office joins the Hercule faceFirst under Work, beside Intake. Home stays Threads: round 1's ask to make the office home is withdrawn.
  • 3A stored lookAn optional look { hue, shape, accessory } on workflows and Assistants, so a rename keeps the face. Without it the look is derived from the name.
  • 4A Run wears its workflow's faceA session a workflow started draws that workflow's character. The data exists; the rule needs writing down.
  • 5Five themes, System, and a glass levelThe theme row lists Whitehaven, Styles, Orient Express, Nile, End House and System, beside a glass level from 0 to 100. Both per browser.
  • 6Batch answers on a Topic pile"Start Fix bug on 9" needs a rule: one operation, or nine Bound Actions sharing one approval (spec 10).
  • 7Answered anywhere clears everywhereADR 0027 already resolves a decision wherever it is answered. The desktop app's notifications and dock badge must be withdrawn the same moment.
  • 8No monospace outside code, and six marksThe design language keeps monospace for code, commands and diffs. Queued, cancelled and skipped lose their marks and become words.

Second edition: the first run

What #313 changes. It is not the installer: #308 installs Hercule and starts it at login.

  • 9The desktop app sets Hercule upSpec 17 leaves onboarding to the browser and opens <url>/setup when setup is not complete. The first run replaces both. Spec 14 and spec 15 §7 expected the desktop to reuse the web's setup views; it draws its own, as spec 17 requires.
  • 10Main reads two files in the Hercule HomeThe address and the token from setup-url, or the address from config.toml once setup is done, and nothing else. The token crosses the bridge once, is never stored, and is read again on Try again, because each start of Hercule mints a new one.
  • 11On the desktop, the first run is a sequenceSpec 14 has no "get started" checklist and derives every step. The desktop runs four steps in order, with Skip. The app keeps its place in its own settings file, so the controller stores nothing new.
