The sidebar, regrouped

You work in crews now. The sidebar still lists threads one by one.

More and more, one piece of work is two or three threads. You start a thread, and it starts a partner: Claude migrates the endpoints, Codex tries to break the migration. The lead writes the partner a better brief than you would, so this is how work will keep arriving.

The sidebar does not know any of this. Every thread is a row like any other, sorted by when it started. Start three pieces of work and you get eight rows that nothing ties together. Below: what a full design team would do with that, the brief that came out of it, and four directions that each organize the list by something different: the crew, the lineage, the product, and time.   1. The list doubles.Every partner is a full 52px row. Partners push lone threads behind each project's "more" row, so adding a reviewer can hide unrelated work.
  2. A pair falls apart.Rows sort by when each thread started. "Fix 3-D Secure checkout" started at 09:02 and its review at 09:30, so the cart work, started at 09:19, lands between them.
  3. The rows do not say whose they are.A partner's title repeats its lead's in other words, and at 272px it is cut before the words that differ. Line 2 is the branch, or only the machine when the controller named the branch.
  4. What needs you hides in plain sight."Check payout totals against Stripe" asks how to count refunds. Waiting on you lists it, but nothing says it is the payout report's checker, so the question arrives without its context.
  5. The header already knows.Its tabs list the threads of the open workspace. The sidebar is the one place that cannot tell a crew from a crowd.

The questionHow does the sidebar show pieces of work, not just threads, so you always know which row belongs to which piece of work, without making a lone thread cost more than it does today?

The jobs the sidebar is hired for

The product owner's frame for every critique. Each direction is scored against these at the end.

FindWhen I want to get back to a piece of work, I want to find it by what it is, not by which of its threads I was in.
AttendWhen any thread needs me, I want to see it and reach it in one move, even if its piece of work is folded away.
OverseeWhen I glance at the sidebar, I want to know what is moving, what is stuck, and what is done.
SwitchWhen I am in a piece of work, I want to move between its lead and its partners without going back to the list.
FinishWhen a piece of work is done, I want to put all of it away at once.

The questions, as the team framed them

  • make the piece of work, not the thread, the thing you find?
  • keep a lead and its partners together without doubling the list?
  • let what needs you reach you, even from a partner tucked away?
  • keep a lone thread exactly as light as today?
  • let grouping and sorting change later, without redesigning the row?
  • show the conversation between the threads, which nobody shows today?

The words that frame the problem

Each word names a choice the four directions make differently.

The degenerate caseA piece of work with one thread. It must look exactly like today's row, or every lone thread pays for the crews.
Containment or connectionBox the crew together (a common region), or draw a line between them (connectedness). Case Files contains; Lineage connects.
Roll-upOne status standing for several threads. The rule: the most urgent wins, and a click goes to the thread that speaks.
Implicit groupingGrouping by a key the system already knows (who started whom), so you never drag anything.
Information scentThe cues that tell you a row is the one you want: a role, an agent, a branch, a face, a diff.
Progressive disclosureThe piece of work first, its threads on demand. Every direction picks a different "on demand".
Spatial memoryYou remember where a row was. A list that re-sorts under you breaks it, which is why rows hold still.
Pre-attentive attributesHue, shape, motion and position, seen before reading. Crew Bureau already spends them: hue is who, a pose is state, marigold is you.
A lensA grouping or sorting over the same atoms. The piece of work is the atom; a lens only decides where it sits.
The brief

One shared model, four ways to show it

The four directions disagree about how a piece of work looks. They agree about what it is. That model is small, and Hercule already stores most of it.

Lead and partner

A thread you start is a lead. A thread started by a session is a partner of that session's lead. A thread you start with the header's + joins that thread's lead. Always one level deep.

It is already stored

sessions.created_by records who started every session. Every input records its actor, so the messages between threads are stored too. Neither is exposed yet.

A role

A partner gets a one-word role ("review", "tests"), written once from its brief, as a title is. It says what the partner adds instead of repeating its lead's title.

Who speaks for the group

Waiting, then failed, then working, then idle. The most urgent thread sets the group's status, and a click on that status opens it.

A group of one is a thread

A lone thread draws exactly as today. Group chrome appears only when a second thread joins.

Finish together

Marking a lead done offers its partners too, with one Undo. A partner can still be marked done alone.

Not the workspace

"Tidy checkout CSS" and "Audit checkout for screen readers" share a main workspace and have nothing to do with each other. The checker of the payout report works on another machine.

Lenses stay open

Group by project, status, machine or nothing; sort by started, activity or name. The piece of work is the atom a lens moves. Every direction shows one other lens.

Principles

  1. One piece of work reads as one thing.You see what belongs together without reading every title.
  2. What needs you always rises.A folded group, a quiet partner, another machine: a waiting Request reaches the surface, and one move reaches it.
  3. A lone thread costs nothing.No extra chrome, height or words.
  4. Nothing moves under the pointer.A partner joining or a status changing never shifts the row you are about to click.
  5. Say it once.The piece of work is named once. A partner is named by what it adds.
  6. Grouping is a lens, not a wall.The model survives any grouping or sorting.
  7. Crew Bureau.Color is who. State is a pose and a mark. Places get tints. Marigold means you.
  8. Cheap while idle.No polling, no timers while nothing changes, nothing per streamed token. Each direction states its cost.

The recommendation, and your pick

Your pick (23 October): Workbench, without the diffs. The faces of Case Files were too much of a distraction in the sidebar. Round 2 refines Workbench: Workbench, refined →. My recommendation below is kept as it was written.

Build Case Files as the sidebar. Take Workbench's findings into the thread.

Your two complaints were that the list doubles and that you cannot tell which row belongs to which piece of work. Case Files answers both with one move: a piece of work is one row, and a partner is a face on it, named by its role. Lineage softens the first complaint but keeps a row per thread. Workbench and Tracks also give one row per piece of work, but spend line 2 on the product or on time, and lose who is on it.

It also spends what only Hercule has. Every thread is a colleague with a face, and a waiting partner raises its hand on its case's row before you read a word. It is the cheapest of the four: who started whom and the messages between threads are already stored. And a partner joining never moves the list.

Workbench's findings in the margin and its duet are not a sidebar idea. They answer a different job, reviewing, and they work beside any sidebar. They are the best answer to your actual pattern, Claude building while Codex tries to break it, so they belong on the roadmap right after the sidebar.

Take from the others

  • From Lineage: a partner is named by its role and agent everywhere, Waiting on you included ("check · Payout report for September"). Keep its kin tag for the day a lens splits a case, such as grouping by machine.
  • From Workbench: the case's branch, changes and PR in the case's foot, and later the findings margin and the duet.
  • From Tracks: a crew never shares a hue (a partner takes the free hue farthest from its crew's), and the day sheet as a later overview beside the Office, not as the row.

In this order

  1. The model. Expose sessions.created_by as startedBy, store the lead for a thread started with the header's +, write a role per partner. Every direction needs this, so it is safe to start now.
  2. Case rows, the crew as tabs, and Close the case.
  3. The exchange line and the case notes.
  4. Findings in the margin. This needs a reviewer to report findings as data (file, line, severity), not prose.
  5. The duet, then the day sheet.

For you to decide

  • Is "case" the word? CONTEXT.md needs one name for a lead and its partners, and "Task" is taken.
  • Should a thread you start with the header's + always join that case, or should the draft ask?
  • May review partners get an output schema for findings?
