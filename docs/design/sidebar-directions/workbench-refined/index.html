Direction 3 · round 2

3Workbench, refined

One row per piece of work. It says what waits on you, and opens into its threads when you ask.

Rogier picked Workbench as the cleanest sidebar, without the change counts, and asked two hard questions. What happens to the dots at ten threads, if they must be clickable inside a popover? And what happens to the header, when real thread titles are long and three of them already reach the buttons?

This round answers both with real-length titles and a ten-thread piece of work. The dots become a summary, never more than three, and one button that unfolds the bench in place. The hover card stays what today's is: something to read, never something to click. And the header names threads by their role, keeps the open and the waiting ones in view, and puts the rest behind a real menu.

What changed since round 1

Less on the row, nothing that floats and asks to be clicked

  • No change counts, in the sidebar or the header. The PR stays, without numbers.
  • Line 2 says what waits on you when anything on the bench does: "review asks · 409 from Stripe: error or retry?". Otherwise the PR or the branch, as before.
  • No faces in the sidebar but the ones Waiting on you already draws today.
  • The dots are a summary: at most three, then "+n". They are no longer buttons one by one, and they lose the open-thread ring.
  • Reaching a thread means unfolding the bench, with the dots or with →. Each thread is then a 32px line you click.
  • The hover card stays read-only and inert, as today's. It lists the crew and says how to unfold.
  • The header has three variants, drawn with real-length titles and a ten-thread bench, at 1440 and at 1100px.
  • Finish crossfades over the status word on hover, as today's Done does.
The row

Round 1 beside round 2

The same morning, the same crop. Round 2 drops the counts and the open-thread ring, and spends line 2 on the one thing worth reading there: what waits on you. A lone thread is today's row, unchanged.

The waiting line

Line 2 says what the bench is waiting for

While any thread of a bench waits on you, line 2 gives its question, in the marigold ink that always means you: "check asks · Settlement or payout date?". The role says which thread asks, the glyph is that thread's provider, and the status word above says whether it is a Question or an Approval.

When the lead itself waits, line 2 is just its question: "Run git push?". When nothing waits, it goes back to the PR, "#1287 Draft ✓", or the branch, or the machine.

So the most useful sentence on the row is the one you can act on, and you no longer have to find the partner to learn what it wants. Waiting on you, at the top, reads the same way: the bench's name, then the role and its question.

The dots rule

Never more than three dots

  • 1 threadToday's row, exactly. No dots, no chevron, no new word.
  • 2 or 3 threadsOne dot per thread, the most urgent first: waiting, working, quiet.
  • More than 3The three most urgent, then the rest as a number: +7. The cluster's width stays under 50px at any crew size.
  • Not buttonsA single dot is never a target. The whole cluster is one button, which unfolds the bench; a chevron appears beside it on hover and on focus.
  • No ringWhich thread is open lives in the header, and in the unfolded lines. The row only says the bench is open.

The colors are the status words': marigold waits on you, the accent's ink works, an empty ring rests. Its accessible name reads the counts: "10 threads: 1 waiting on you, 3 working, 6 quiet. Unfold".

Unfold, not a clickable popover

The sidebar opens in place. Nothing floats.

Rogier's worry was right: a hover card full of targets is not how today's card, or any Mac sidebar, behaves. It hides when the pointer leaves, so reaching into it is a race. So the card stays inert, and the way to a thread is the way every sidebar already works: open the item, and its children are lines in the list.

Why unfold wins

It is the gesture people already know from Finder, Mail and every tree. It works with the keyboard (→ and ←), with VoiceOver (a group with an expanded state), and with any number of threads. And it never covers the window.

What stays calm

Unfolding is memory only, so a relaunch folds everything. Opening a thread from the header never unfolds a bench or scrolls the list. A partner joining adds a dot, never a line, unless you unfolded the bench yourself.

The header problem

Three real titles already reach the buttons

Today's header gives every thread of the workspace a tab with its whole title. Titles are long in real use, so with ten threads the tabs run off the window, and the header's own buttons are pushed out of it.

The three header variants

Roles, one switcher, or shrinking tabs

Each variant works on every state of the screen: add &header=a, b or c to its address. All three keep the same right-hand cluster, Duet, the PR, Finish and More, and end at least 24px before it, laid out again when the window is resized. Each is drawn here at 1440 and at 1100px.

A · Roles, then overflow

The crumb carries the bench's name, cut at about 280px. Tabs are named by role, with today's marks. What does not fit goes behind "+n", but the open tab and any waiting one always stay.

B · One switcher

The crumb, then one pill for the open thread, "lead · Claude Code ▾", with the bench's dots beside it. Its menu lists the whole crew. The same width at any crew size.

C · Shrinking tabs

Today's tabs by title, shrinking like a browser's: every tab gets the same width, between 72 and 200px. Below 72, the rest go behind "+n".

The recommendation

A: name threads by role, and let the rest overflow.

A role is short because it says what the thread adds, not what the whole piece of work is: "review", "tests", "API docs". The bench's name is said once, in the crumb. So five or six tabs fit where today's three did not, and the ones that matter never leave: the open one, and the one that waits on you.

B is the calmest at any size, but it hides who is working until you open its menu, and switching always takes two moves. C is what browsers do, and at ten threads it turns every title into three letters; it is the best fallback for a crew with no roles at all. A keeps today's tabs, and only changes what they say.

Roles

Where a role comes from

Everything above leans on roles, so they have to be dependable. They come from three places, in this order.

1 · The lead names it

The usual case. The lead starts the partner with hercule session spawn --role review, so the role is the lead's own word for the job. All eight named partners on the migration got theirs this way.

2 · A Text Generation writes it

For a thread Rogier adds with the header's +, a Text Generation writes one from its first message, the way a title is written: sessions/thread.role.

3 · The title, as a fallback

Until a role exists, the thread shows its title, cut short: at 160px on a tab, and to the line's width when unfolded. The two threads added with + on the migration show this, in the menu above.

What it takes

What it needs, and what it no longer needs

Needs
  • The lead. Expose sessions.created_by on Session as startedBy, and store leadSessionId on a thread started with the header's +.
  • Roles. A --role flag on hercule session spawn, stored on the session; and a Text Generation, sessions/thread.role, for threads that start without one.
  • The waiting line. Nothing new: it is the Request's one-line question, which Waiting on you already shows.
  • Spec. Spec 17 §The sidebar (one row per bench, the waiting line, the dots rule, unfolding), the thread header (role tabs and the "+n" menu), and the hover card's list of the crew.
No longer needs
  • Change counts. No runner git diff --shortstat, nothing reported on the workspace, nothing to keep fresh.
  • The PR state is optional. When the GitHub Connection gives it, line 2 and the header show "#1287 Draft"; without it, the branch. Nothing else depends on it.
  • Clickable popovers. No hover card with targets in it, and no focus traps outside the list.
What it costs

In ADR 0037's terms

  • Processes: none new.
  • Memory: fewer rows than today, one per bench. An unfolded bench adds a 32px line per shown thread, and "n quiet" keeps a ten-thread bench to five lines. The hover card is the one card today's sidebar already has.
  • Work while idle: none. No timers, no polling.
  • Work per streamed token: none. A row redraws when a thread's status changes, not when it streams. The header lays its tabs out when it is drawn, when a status changes, and when the window is resized, once a frame at most.
  • Bundle bytes: the bench row, the dots and the unfolded lines in the shell's chunk; the tab layout and the menu in the thread's chunk. The duet of round 1 is not part of this round.
  • Measured by: the perf script's sidebar scenario with 500 threads as benches, with one ten-thread bench unfolded, and the window dragged from 1440 to 1100 to check the tabs' layout stays inside the frame budget.
For the grilling session

Open questions

The noun

Is "bench" the word? It reads well on Finish ("finish the bench") and in the docs, but nobody says it today. The alternatives are a piece of work, a crew, or no noun at all, with the lead's title standing for it.

Does + always join?

The header's + starts a thread on the open bench. Is that always what you mean, or do you sometimes want a stranger in the same worktree? If so, + needs a second choice: "On this bench" or "Alone, in this worktree".

The role flag

Should hercule session spawn take --role, and should the lead be told to use it? Without it, every partner waits for a Text Generation, and its tab shows the title until then.
