2Lineage

Every thread keeps its row. Kinship is drawn, not boxed, so it survives any sort.

When a thread starts a partner, Lineage puts the partner right under it and joins the two with a line in the lead's own color. The partner's row says only what it adds: review · Codex · Working. You see which threads belong together before you read a single title, and every thread is still a row you click, as in Claude Code, Codex or T3 Code.

Because the relation lives on each row and not in a box, it survives whatever grouping comes later. Group by status and the families split up, yet every partner still says whose it is, and one hover lights the whole family across the list.

Anatomy

One line, one color, no boxes

The 3-D Secure work, with its review open. Drawn at one and a half times its size.

  1. The lead keeps today's row

    Same 52px, same title, same status word, same branch. Nothing about a lead changes when it starts a partner, except that a line now leaves from under its provider's mark.

  2. The line is the lead's color

    In Crew Bureau, color is who. The lead is who started the family, so the line wears the lead's signature hue, the same hue its face wears in the Office. Lines of other families are a step quieter than the open one's, so you always see where you are.

  3. A partner says what it adds

    Its face sits on the line, then its role, its agent and its status: review · Codex · Working. The role is written once by a Text Generation from the brief the lead gave it, as a title is. Its full title is one hover away.

  4. Quiet partners fold into the knot

    A partner with nothing going on folds into a ring on the line: research · quiet · 20m. One click opens the knot. A family of four with two idle partners takes three rows.

  5. Waiting on you says whose it is

    A waiting partner is named by its role and its lead, check · Payout report for September, so you know what you are answering for before you open it.

  6. A lone thread draws nothing

    No line, no knot, no indent. "Audit checkout for screen readers" shares a main workspace with "Tidy checkout CSS" and they stay strangers, because sharing a workspace is not sharing the work.

  7. The header follows the family

    The tabs are the family, not the workspace: the lead by its title, each partner by its role. A checker in a scratch workspace on another machine is still a tab.

The huh

Group the list by status. The families split up. The kinship does not.

Every other tool that groups threads puts them in a box: a task, a space, a folder. A box decides the grouping for you, and the day you want to sort by status the box is in the way. Lineage never boxes. The relation is carried by each row: a dot in the lead's color and the lead's name.

So the 3-D Secure work can sit in three groups at once (its lead waiting on you, its review working, its research quiet), and one hover on any of them lights all three. The same holds for any lens Hercule adds later: by machine, by provider, flat by last activity.

The line carries the conversation

When one thread messages another, you see it travel

A lead briefs its partner, the partner reports back. Hercule already stores every one of those messages. Lineage shows each one as a short signal that runs down or up the line, once. No badge, no counter, no sound: just enough to tell you the pair is talking. Under Reduce Motion it does not run.

The states

The moments that matter

Each frame is the live page. Open one to click around: rows open threads, the funnel switches the grouping, a knot opens, and Done on a lead finishes its family.

What it takes

What it needs, and what it costs

What it needs

  • A thread's lead. Expose sessions.created_by on Session as startedBy: the controller already stores who started each session. A thread a session started is a partner of that session's lead.
  • The + in the header records a lead. A thread Rogier starts from a thread's header stores that thread's lead (leadSessionId), because a shared workspace is not proof.
  • A role. One or two words from a Text Generation over the brief (sessions/thread.role), written once, like a title.
  • Exchanges for the signal. Inputs whose actor is session:<id>, already stored. The list needs only to hear that one arrived.
  • Spec 17 changes. Partners sort under their lead. Projects sort by their newest lead, so a partner never reorders the list. The five-per-project cap counts families. Done on a lead marks the family done.

What it costs (ADR 0037)

  • Processes: none.
  • Memory: a role string per partner, and one short row instead of a tall one. The list mounts about the same number of rows: partner rows are 32px, knots 24px.
  • Idle: nothing. No timer, no polling. The line is CSS on the rows, drawn with them.
  • Per streamed token: nothing. A signal runs only when an exchange is stored: one transform animation on one element, none under Reduce Motion.
  • Bundle bytes: small. The family sort and the knot rule are pure functions in @hercule/client-core; the rows add a few CSS rules.
  • To measure: the perf script's sidebar scenario with 500 threads in 200 families, to check that sorting partners under leads stays inside a frame.
Critique

What the team pushed back on

"A tree in a sidebar looks busy."

That is why there is one line per family, one hue, no boxes, no chevrons and no headers. A lone thread draws nothing, so the tree only appears where there is a family.

"Two leads can share a hue."

Eight hues will collide: the payout report and the Result migration are both peach. The hue is a hint, not an id. Position carries the family in the project view, and the kin tag always says the lead's name.

"Partners still cost height."

A partner is 32px, not 52, and a quiet one folds into the knot at 24. In this morning's world Lineage shows all 16 threads in the height today's list needs for 14.

"Why not fold the whole family into one row?"

Because then the partners are a click away, and the lens has nothing to move. Case Files makes that bet. Lineage bets you want every thread one click away, and only the quiet ones folded.

"A partner joining moves the list."

Only when your pointer is not in it. While you are in the list, a new partner waits as a +1 on its lead and slides in when you leave. Projects never reorder for a partner.

"What if a partner starts its own partner?"

It joins the same lead. A family is always one level deep, so the line never forks twice and the indent never grows.
