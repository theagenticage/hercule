4Tracks

Time organizes the list. Every piece of work is a lane, and every thread a track in it, running up to now.

Today a lead and its partners are three rows that nothing ties together. In Tracks they are one row, exactly as tall as today's. Line 1 is still the title and the status word. Line 2 becomes the last hour of that work: one track per thread, in the thread's own color, ending at now.

So you stop reading the sidebar and start seeing it. Which work is moving, which partner never got going, which one has been waiting on you for ten minutes, what happened while you were in a meeting. The words are today's. The picture under them is new.

Anatomy

How to read a lane

The 3-D Secure lane, at three times its size. Seven things to know, and after a day you will not need any of them.

  1. Line 1 is today's row

    The lead's title, and the status of whoever needs you most: Approval, because the lead wants to push. Click the word to open that thread.

  2. One track per thread, lead on top

    Each track is drawn in its thread's own color, the color of its face. The faces in the header's tabs are the legend.

  3. Beads are turns, hairlines are idle

    A thick bead is a turn that ran. A hairline is a thread that is loaded and free. A dotted line is a thread asleep.

  4. Partners branch off their lead

    A partner's track leaves its lead's at the minute it was started. A tick joins two tracks where one thread messaged the other.

  5. Marigold is you

    From the moment a thread started waiting on you, its track turns marigold and runs into the waiting mark at now.

  6. The caret is where you were

    It marks when you last looked at the lane. What came before is faint, what came after is bright. The open lane has no caret: you are looking at it.

  7. The right edge is now

    Once a minute every strip slides one step left. Rows never move.

The right edge is now

One step a minute, and nothing redrawn

In this frame the clock runs at thirty times its speed, a minute every two seconds. Every strip slides one step left as one transform. A working track already runs past now and is cut at the edge, so it grows into now for free. A turn starting or ending redraws one strip; a streamed token changes nothing.

At its real speed this happens once a minute, with a 0.7 second ease, and as a jump under Reduce Motion. Rows never move.

The huh

You can see your morning.

Every agentic tool shows you a list of words: titles, statuses, ages. None shows you rhythm. Which work has been stuck for an hour. Which partner never started. That the reviewer and the implementer have been passing messages for twenty minutes. That nothing moved while you were away, or that everything did.

Tracks draws it once, in the row you already have. Press ⌘⇧T, or click the ruler, and the lanes spread out into a day sheet over the main pane: every thread of every piece of work on one clock, like a film's call sheet.

The states

Every state the brief asks for

The same world in every direction. Each frame is live: hover a track, click a lane, press ⌘⇧T.

Grouping is a lens

The lane is the atom, so any grouping works

Every lens moves whole lanes. A piece of work never splits across groups, because its threads are drawn inside one row. The time axis is the same under every lens, so a lane reads the same wherever it lands.

Today's sidebar has no lens at all. Adding one later is a menu and a sort, not a redesign of the row.

What it needs

Two new reads and one change to faces

On top of the shared model every direction needs (a thread's startedBy, the lead of a thread started with the header's +, a partner's role, and the exchanges, which are already stored).

Activity spans

When each thread's turns ran, for the last few hours: a start and an end per turn, derived from the session's stored stream (the turn's start and end events). Read once with the session list for the threads in view, then kept live from the status updates the app already receives: a turn starting or ending is one new span.

Last looked

When Rogier last had a thread of each piece of work open. The client keeps it in localStorage, as it keeps the sidebar's layout, because it belongs to this Mac. The phone keeps its own.

A crew never shares a color

Faces are seeded from the thread, so two threads of one piece of work can wear the same hue. This morning the 3-D Secure research and review would both be sky. A track that carries its thread's color needs a rule: a partner's face takes the hue farthest from its crew's, decided once when it starts. Amends spec 17's "A thread's face is seeded by its session id". It helps every direction, and the Office.

Spec 17, §The sidebar

The thread row becomes the lane row. Its second line is the strip; the place (branch, machine) moves to the hover card and the composer's lip. The ruler, the day sheet (⌘⇧T) and the lens menu are new. The 5-per-project cap counts lanes.

What it costs

Cheap while idle, nothing per token

In ADR 0037's terms. To be measured with a new perf scenario: 500 threads, the minute tick and a turn ending, against rule 2's bar of at most 2 ms of style and layout in a frame.

Risks the team raised

And the answers

"Data viz in a sidebar is unfamiliar."

Line 1 is today's row, untouched, so everything a Codex or Claude Code user reads first is where they expect it. The strip replaces only line 2, which today is often just a machine's name.

"Hairlines are too subtle."

They are meant to be quiet. Only three things speak up: now, marigold, and the caret. The beads are a step darker than a face on light themes and its fill on dark ones.

"Where did the branch go?"

To the hover card and the composer's lip. The row now says when and who instead of where. For the threads Rogier runs, "where" was mostly a generated branch or a machine.

"Which track is which?"

Lead on top, partners in the order they started, each in its face's color, with the faces in the header as the legend. Hover a track and the card names it; click it and that thread opens.

"A list that is always moving is distracting."

The strips step once a minute, with a 0.7 second ease, and jump under Reduce Motion. Rows never move; a partner joining changes a strip, not the list.

"A lane with six threads?"

The strip draws four tracks at 3px. Beyond four, quiet partners would fold into their lead's hairline. Not drawn here: no lane this morning has more than three.
