1Case Files

One piece of work, one row. The threads working on it ride along as faces.

You start a thread, and it starts a reviewer. Today that is two rows with two different titles, and each lands wherever its start time puts it. Case Files makes the piece of work the row: a case. Its crew is drawn on the row as faces, in their poses, and the last thing they said to each other sits right beside them.

A case of one is today's row, unchanged. A partner joining adds a face, never a row, so the list never moves under you. And when the work is done, Close puts the whole crew away at once.

Anatomy

A case row says the work's name once, then shows its crew.

Every part answers one of the five questions Rogier asks the sidebar. Hover any part of the live frame to try it: a face shows its member, the exchange line opens the case notes, the chevron opens the file.

1 2 3 4 5 6 7

  1. The case's name, said once

    A case takes its lead's title. The partners do not repeat it: inside the case they are named by their role, such as review, research or check.

  2. Whoever is most urgent speaks for the case

    The status slot is the most urgent member's: waiting, then working, then idle. Question on Payout report is the checker's, not the lead's. Under the pointer, Close takes the slot's place, as Mark done does today.

  3. The crew strip

    Every member's face, in its pose, the lead first. A member that waits raises its hand with the marigold palm and sits on top, so it is never covered. Each face is a door: one click opens that member.

  4. The exchange line

    The last message between members. review then text is a report to the lead. → tests is the lead's brief to a partner. When a partner waits, its question takes the line instead, in marigold.

  5. The case file

    Opens with the chevron, with →, or by itself when you open any member. One short row per member: role, agent, status, and the question when it waits. The file and its row share one tint, so they read as one thing.

  6. The case's foot

    What the case has produced, its branch and its changes, and Close, which marks the whole crew done with one Undo.

  7. A case of one

    A thread that works alone draws exactly as today's row, with its provider's mark and branch on the second line. The crew strip appears only when a second thread joins.

The part nobody has done

You can read your agents' conversation without opening a single thread.

Every message one thread sends another is already stored by Hercule, as an input whose actor is a session. No tool shows it. Case Files puts the latest one on the row, and hovering it opens the case notes: the whole exchange as a tape of telegrams, who sent what to whom, and when.

And the faces do the status work before you read a word. The raised hand with the marigold palm is the one that needs you, whichever member of the case it is.

What it takes

What it needs, and what it costs.

Data and spec
  • startedBy on Session. The controller already stores who started a session (sessions.created_by). The contract exposes it, so a thread started by a session joins that session's case.
  • The lead of a thread added by hand. A thread started from the header's + stores the case it joins (leadSessionId), because a shared workspace is not proof.
  • A role per partner. One Text Generation at start, from the brief (sessions/thread.role), as a thread's title is written today.
  • The last exchange per case. Inputs whose actor is a session of the same case, already stored. The list needs only the newest per case; the case notes read the rest on hover.
  • Spec 17 §The sidebar lists cases instead of threads, defines who speaks for a case, adds Close case (marking each member done, with one Undo), and turns the header's tabs into the crew.
  • CONTEXT.md gains Case: a lead thread and its partners. Avoid: group, bundle, stack.
Cost, in ADR 0037's terms
  • Processes: none new.
  • Memory: one short string per case for the exchange line, and two or three small face SVGs per case row. The list stays virtualized, and it mounts fewer rows than today, because there are fewer rows.
  • Work while idle: none. No timer, no polling, no live topic of its own.
  • Work per streamed token: none. Line 2 changes only when an exchange lands or a member's pose changes.
  • Reads: the newest exchange per case rides on the session list's read. The case notes read one case's exchanges on hover, a handful of rows.
  • Bundle bytes: the case row, the case file and the notes card in the shell's chunk, a few KB. The case rules (lead, spokesperson) live in client-core with their tests.
The team's doubts

What the team pushed back on, and the answers.

"A face is not a name."

Faces are seeded at random, so nobody learns them. They do not have to be learned: they say who is working and who is waiting. The case's name and the members' roles carry the names.

"Another noun."

Case is the Bureau's own word, and an honest one: a case has a crew. It takes no meaning from Task, which is intent, or Workspace, which is a place.

"The exchange line is cut short."

At 272px it is a scent, not the message. The case notes hold the whole tape, one hover away, and a wider sidebar shows more of the line.

"Partners get hidden."

Never the one that needs you. Its face raises a hand, its question takes line 2, and Waiting on you lists it under the case's name. The file also opens by itself on the case you are in.

"Close is a bigger hammer than Mark done."

One Undo brings the whole crew back, and the shelf keeps them together. The model still lets a single member be marked done; this drawing shows only Close.

"18px faces are small."

They overlap like people standing close, each cut by a ring in the row's own color, and the waiting one is drawn on top. The open case's file shows each member at a full row.
