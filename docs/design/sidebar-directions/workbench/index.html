3Workbench

The sidebar lists the work. The window seats the crew.

You think in pieces of work: the 3-D Secure fix, the Result type migration. You do not think in threads. So in Workbench a row of the sidebar is a bench: one piece of work, named by what it produces, with its branch or pull request and its changes on the second line. Its threads never get rows of their own. A few small dots at the end of the row say who is on it, and which one needs you.

The threads sit in the window, where you work with them. The header's tabs become a crew bar: the lead under the work's name, each partner under its role. Seat two of them side by side, and their transcripts run on one clock, with every message between them drawn as an arrow. When the reviewer finds something, the finding lands in the margin, beside the file it is about.

Anatomy

A row says what the work made. The window says who made it.

The bench row keeps today's 52px and its two lines, so it reads at once. What changes is what the lines say.

  1. The work's name

    A bench is named by its pull request once it has one, and by its lead's title before that. The tidy-CSS work reads "Remove 212 unused checkout CSS rules", because that is what it became.

  2. What it produced

    Line 2 is the branch, or the pull request with its state and checks, then the changes in diff colors: fix/3ds-eu-cards +48 −12, or #1287 Draft ✓ +412 −388. Work with no checkout shows its machine, as today's row does.

  3. The crew dots

    One dot per thread, the lead first, in the order of the seats. Marigold waits on you, green works, an empty ring rests. A ring around a dot marks the thread open in the window. Each dot is a button.

  4. One status for the bench

    The word at the end is the most urgent thread's: Approval, Question, Working, or an age. Click the word and you land on the thread that said it. Click the row and the bench opens where you left it.

  5. Waiting on you names the work

    "Payout report for September · check Settlement or payout date?" says which work asks and which role is asking, so you never have to remember that "Check payout totals against Stripe" belongs to the payout report.

  1. Seats, not tabs

    The lead's seat carries the work's name. Each partner's seat carries its role: research, review. Say it once: "Adversarial review of the 3-D Secure fix" becomes "review", because the bench already says which fix.

  2. ⌥1 to ⌥4

    Seats switch with a key, in the order of the dots. A seat that waits on you calls you twice in marigold when the bench opens, then stays still.

  3. The product on the right

    The pull request, the changes, and Finish, which marks the whole crew done in one move. Spec 17 left "Changes +48 −12" out of the header because nothing reported it; the bench brings it back.

The huh

Watch your agents talk to each other.

Press Duet, and the lead and its reviewer sit side by side, on one clock. The brief the lead sent at 09:30 is an arrow across, in the lead's color. The reviewer's first finding at 09:38 is an arrow back, in the reviewer's. You read the loop between them like a sequence diagram, and see at once who is waiting on whom: the lead on you, the reviewer on nobody.

And when a review finds something, the finding does not stay buried in the reviewer's transcript. It is pinned in the margin of the lead's thread, beside the changed file it is about, with a line to the row. Every message between threads is already stored, as an input whose actor is another session. Nobody shows it. Workbench does.

What it takes

What it needs, and what it costs

Needs
  • The lead. Expose sessions.created_by on Session as startedBy, and store leadSessionId on a thread started from the crew bar's +. The bench is the lead and its partners; the workspace is only evidence.
  • Roles. A Text Generation, sessions/thread.role, writes a partner's role once from its brief, as a thread's title is written.
  • Change counts (new read). The runner reports the checkout's git diff --shortstat against its base on the workspace record, when the checkout changes. Pushed, never polled.
  • Pull request state (new read). The PR's number, state and checks for the bench's branch, from the GitHub Connection's events, kept on the workspace.
  • Exchanges. Already stored: inputs whose actor is a session. The duet reads them from the two transcripts it already loads.
  • Findings. No new record. A partner's report that names a file:line the lead changed is pinned there, matched by the client.
  • Spec. Spec 17 §The sidebar (one row per bench, the product line, crew dots, five benches per project), the thread header (the crew bar replaces the tabs), and two new sections: the duet, and findings in the margin.
Costs, in ADR 0037's terms
  • Processes: none new.
  • Memory: the sidebar mounts fewer rows, one per bench (10 instead of 16 here). The duet mounts a second transcript, which roughly doubles a thread's memory while it is open; closing it frees it, and only one duet is open at a time. A pinned finding is a handful of elements.
  • Work while idle: none. No timers. The seat's marigold call runs twice and stops. Change counts and PR state arrive as pushes.
  • Work per streamed token: none in the sidebar, because dots change with status, not tokens. In the duet, two transcripts stream as they would in two windows; the arrows are laid out once per exchange and on resize, never per token.
  • Bundle bytes: the bench row and dots in the shell's chunk; the duet and the margin in the thread's chunk, on first use.
  • Measured by: the perf script's sidebar scenario with 500 threads as benches, and a new duet scenario: memory with two transcripts of 500 rows, and frame time while both stream.
The team's objections

Risks the team raised, and the answers

"Reaching a partner takes two moves." UX expert

One click on its dot opens it. In the window, ⌥ and its number. Waiting on you opens the asking thread directly. And a bench reopens on the seat you left it on.

"Two new reads, for a sidebar." Product owner

They are the work's most useful facts, and spec 17 already wanted "Changes" in the header. Until they land, line 2 falls back to today's branch or machine, and the row still works.

"The duet doubles memory." Lead designer

It is opt-in, one bench at a time, and closing it frees the second transcript. The sidebar pays some of it back, with fewer rows.

"Does hiding the crew hide what needs me?" UX expert

No. The marigold dot, the bench's status word, the "needs you" count on the project, and Waiting on you all rise, and each one is a single click from the thread that asks.

"Work with no branch has no product." Senior designer

It shows its main workspace or machine, as today's row does ("payments-api · build-box-2"), and lands under "Nothing to ship" in the PR lens.

"What if a partner works elsewhere?" Junior designer

It is still on the bench, because the bench follows who started whom, not the workspace. The payout report's checker runs in a scratch workspace on build-box-2; the bench card says so.
