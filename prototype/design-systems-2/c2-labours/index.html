IIWhy this one

The crew you liked, given the name's other root.

Over the original

Round 1 had the prettiest office and the cutest crew, but it opened on the office, spoke in five button colours and gave the crew no link to the name. Labours keeps the crew, the office and Waiting on you, and adds threads first, one olive answer in one column, and a Hercules in every figure.

Over Bureau

Bureau reads Hercule as Poirot: lacquer, brass, Deco type, tubes and raised rectangles. Labours reads it as Hercules: stone, olive and terracotta, a courtyard with an aqueduct, and full pills whose material comes from the glass level.

What it trades away

Hercules is the name's second root, not Poirot, and a myth invites theme-park kitsch, so each area gets one or two classical cues at most. The heroes are a touch less playful than round 1's creatures.

IVThe system

One number for glass, one meaning per colour, one column for answers.

Six rules carry the whole design. Everything below follows from them.

  1. Threads firstA new user meets a place to run threads. The office, Intake and the rest are one tab away.
  2. Colour means one thingOlive is the suggested step, gold is you, brick is failure. Nothing glows.
  3. One filled buttonThe suggested answer is the only olive pill in a group, and always comes first.
  4. Glass only floatsOnly layers over content are glass, and one number sets how much.
  5. Who, then whatA hue and a prop say who a colleague is. A pose and a mark say what it is doing.
  6. One face for the UIBricolage Grotesque everywhere. Recursive only where the text is code.

Glass

One number, 0 to 100%, 50% by default. Set per browser in Appearance.

What may be glass

  • Layers that float over content: the header pills, the composer and its dock, the queued row, menus, the quick panel, sheets, the phone's tab bar, and the office's floating card.
  • The desktop sidebar, over the window ground.
  • Buttons, as a material: stone at 0, ceramic at 100%.

What never is

  • Rows, cards and the transcript. They are content; glass shows content.
  • Decoration. Nothing sits under glass just to be seen through it: no gradients, no blobs.
  • Unreadable text. Text surfaces stay at least 74% opaque (70% for the shrunk composer), so text keeps 4.5:1.
  • Reduced transparency: when the system asks for it, the level is 0 whatever the slider says.
0%solid

A solid pill, 760px wide. The last message stops at its edge.

50%the default

85% opaque with a 19px blur. The message under it reads as a shape, not as text.

100%the most

70% opaque, 26px blur and a lit top edge. It narrows to 560px so more of the transcript shows.

Shape, depth and motion

Shape

Soft rectangles for surfaces, from 6 to 22px radius. Full pills for everything you press, so a pill always means "you can press this".

Depth

One level of containment at most, separated by space, tone and hairlines. Shadows are soft and warm; glass layers add a light top edge.

Motion

120, 200 and 320ms with a plain ease-out. Only the characters get a little spring: the working tap, the waiting wave, the sleeping z. Reduce motion stops them.

IXSpec changes

What this design asks the spec to change.

Home is already Sessions in spec 14, so threads first needs no change. The office's event flow only draws events the controller already records.

  1. Waiting on you on both faces

    A section on top of the Threads and the Hercule face alike, threads and runs together, the question in one line.

  2. Labels and icons in the Hercule navspec 14, app shell

    Work and System as small labels, the Office as a row under System, and an outline icon on every row. The spec pins "the four entity items carry their glyph, the rest none".

  3. Meta lines and counts in the UI facespec 14, design language

    The spec sets ids, refs, ages and counts in IBM Plex Mono. Labours keeps mono for code, commands, diffs and paths only.

  4. One filled button for the suggested answerspec 14, design language

    This conflicts with the spec: decision affordances are "quiet text-only ghost buttons, never filled buttons on monitoring surfaces", and semantic hues are "never fills, chips, stripes or washes". Labours asks for one olive fill per group.

  5. Six state marks

    Working, waiting on you, done, failed, paused and idle. Queued is the clock icon; cancelled is a word.

  6. A stored look for colleaguesspec 12 §1

    An Assistant and a workflow each keep a look: hue, body and prop. A Run wears its workflow's look. Spec 12 has no field for it today.

  7. Themes and glass

    Five themes plus Follow the system, and a glass level kept per browser.

  8. Batch answers on a Topic pilespec 10 §7.4

    At 10x, one answer for a pile of similar Proposals: Start "Fix bug" on 9, Accept 6 as Tasks.

  9. Answered anywhere clears everywhereADR 0027, spec 10 §7.7

    Already the rule; restated because the quick panel, the menu bar, notifications and the lock screen all rely on it.
