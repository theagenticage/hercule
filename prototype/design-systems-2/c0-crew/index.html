Crew

Your agents are colleagues.

Hercule runs a crowd of agents, and today each one is a line of grey text. Crew gives every session, workflow and Assistant a face: a small character drawn from its name and a hue, whose pose is its state. Focused eyes mean working. A raised marigold hand means it waits on you. Closed eyes, asleep. A bandage, failed. Color always means who; marigold always means you. You read the room the way you read an office: in one glance, before a single word.

Why this one

Most redesigns make rows prettier. Crew changes what a row is.

People spot a face, a raised hand or a slumped posture faster than any icon or word, and Hercule's hard question is exactly that: which of 23 colleagues needs me right now? A face also scales from 16px in a list to a whole office, so one idea carries the sidebar, the lock screen and the 3D floor.

What it trades away

  • It is the most opinionated of the ten. Some will find characters too playful for an ops tool, so the poses must stay calm and professional forever.
  • Eight hues cannot keep 140 colleagues apart. Shape, accessory and name do the rest, and at swarm scale faces give way to counts.
  • A character system is an illustration practice to maintain, not a token swap.

The office

The office is the home screen, not a toy on the side.

It shows the same colleagues, in the same poses, as the sidebar and the list. The office adds one thing words cannot: where everyone is.

  • Rooms are projectsEach room's floor is the project's rug: webshop, payments-api, ops. You find a colleague by place.
  • A desk is a sessionThe screen shows the turn; the character types while it works and slumps when it is idle.
  • Your desk holds the queueWhoever waits on you lines up at your desk, and the decision card answers the first of 3 without leaving the floor.
  • Racks are machinesEach lit slot is a running session. A machine at cap shows it, so placement is visible.
  • Lounge and benchAssistants sit in the lounge; idle workflows wait on the window bench until an event calls them.
  • The list is first classOne toggle turns the floor into a roster: Waiting on you, Working, Idle, with answers inline. On a phone the roster is the Office, under a plan of the rooms.

What this design asks the spec to change

Seven changes, none to the domain's meaning.

  • 1Home is the Office, not SessionsSpec 14 says "Home is Sessions". The All sessions lanes survive as the Office's List view, so nothing is lost; the default changes.
  • 2The sidebar shows places and peopleNav, Waiting on you and Assistants, instead of the Threads and Hercule faces behind a segmented switch (spec 14, App shell).
  • 3A stored lookAn optional look { hue, shape, accessory } on workflows and Assistants, so a rename keeps the face. Without it the look is derived from the name.
  • 4A Run wears its workflow's faceA session a workflow started draws that workflow's character. The data exists; the rule needs writing down.
  • 5Five themes and SystemThe theme row lists Studio, Sunroom, Cocoa, Pine, Plum and System, instead of Light, Dark and System. Still per browser.
  • 6Batch answers on a Topic pile"Start Fix bug on 9" needs a rule: one operation, or nine Bound Actions sharing one approval (spec 10).
  • 7Answered anywhere clears everywhereADR 0027 already resolves a decision wherever it is answered. The desktop app's notifications and dock badge must be withdrawn the same moment.
