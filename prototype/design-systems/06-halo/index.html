Halo

Glance, don't read.

Hercule runs dozens of agents while you do other work. The one question you bring to it is does anything need me? Halo answers that question with a shape. Every session and Proposal is a segment on a ring: green moving, sodium your move, red broke, grey at rest. The ring is the logo, the favicon, the dock icon, the menu bar, the Intake dial and the office's ceiling light. Numerals replace sentences, and answers are solid sodium rows. Five themes; desktop, web and phone share one grammar.

moving your move broke at rest
01

Why this one

The other nine directions give Hercule a better list. Halo bets that a list is the wrong first answer.

The question you bring is almost always "is anything waiting on me?". A ring answers it in the time a list takes to load, and it answers it at 16 pixels as well as at 500. One glyph, learned once, is read in the browser tab, the menu bar, the lock screen and the Intake page. The words are still there, one click or one hover away; they just stop being the first thing you have to read.

  • No hue for anything but state. Projects, sources and people are words and mono marks. If you want webshop blue and ops pink, this is the wrong system.
  • The ring says how many, not which. Three wait and eight work; which three is one hover or one click away.
  • A learning week. Thickness, the notch and hollow pauses are a grammar. The legend in Settings teaches it, but day one is slower than a list.

03

The system

Color is state, shape repeats state, and one ring carries both from 16px to 500px.

Color doctrine

Only state has a hue. When you see color, something is happening; the brighter and thicker it is, the more it needs you. Each state also has a shape, so color is never the only signal. One exception: diff counts keep the +green and -red every developer already reads.

Moving

An agent is working, streaming or running. Full thickness; it shimmers.

Five themes

Every theme keeps the doctrine and changes only the ground. Click one to switch this book and every screen in it. Follow system pairs Daylight and Eclipse.

The office

The office is the halo in three dimensions, so it belongs without a second visual language. It is a view of All sessions (List | Office), not a place of its own: the same records, the same colors, the same counts. It is built for a second monitor you glance at, not for doing the work.

06

At 10x

A swarm is where a ring pays off. A list grows with every session; a ring gets finer. The numeral in the middle stays the one number that matters: how many wait on you.

  • 140 sessions, one ring. Segments thin to ticks; the 14 that wait are still the thick ones, and the notch still points at the oldest.
  • Intake groups by topic. The Now tier stays rows; the other 57 fold into four topics, each with its own ring and one answer for the group.
  • Runners become a grid of rings. Nine runners, 102 of 126 desks, read at once in the rail.
  • The rail says what to do. "Answer them in order": the oldest first, and nothing else needs you.

The first five changes

In this order, so each lands on its own and the app keeps working between them.

  1. Swap the tokens and the fonts

    Replace the Midnight block in packages/ui/src/styles.css with tokens.css (five themes, today's names kept, the aliases for --attn and --live). Replace Onest and IBM Plex Mono in packages/ui/src/fonts with Outfit and Geist Mono (both OFL). Nothing else moves yet.

  2. Add the Halo component, and redraw the marks as rings

    A Halo SVG component in packages/ui/src/marks with the size hinting and the segment grammar, tested like the marks are today. Redraw marks.tsx, turn priority-glyph.tsx into the four-segment gauge, and make logo.tsx the ring. Halo segments come from a client-core function, so the dial, the favicon and the badge can never disagree.

  3. Make answers decisive

    Add a filled sodium variant and an outline variant to primitives/button.tsx, and an answer ledger (answer, then what it does, ⌘↵ on the first) to patterns/patterns.tsx. Use it for the Intake lead card, the Proposal dossier and the permission ask on the composer.

  4. Put the ring in the shell

    In apps/web/src/shell/pulse.tsx the pulse line becomes the halo glance ("3 waiting on you · 8 working · 12 idle"). The shell sets a live favicon and a "(3)" title prefix from the same live topic. theme/theme-selector.tsx offers five themes and Follow system. screens/project-dot.tsx goes.

  5. Rebuild the Intake band

    In apps/web/src/routes/_shell/intake.tsx the headline sentence becomes the dial and the numerals; tiers take their thickness swatch from the dial. Then the thread: the composer folds to glass when you scroll back (screens/composer).

08

What this design asks the spec to change

Eight changes. Each one names the section it would amend.

Spec 14 · Design language
("Register")
Decisions get a filled answer.

Today: "decision affordances are quiet text-only ghost buttons, never filled buttons on monitoring surfaces". Halo: the suggested answer is one filled sodium bar in a ledger that states what each answer does. The others stay outlined.

Spec 14 · Design language
("Semantic hues", "Surfaces")
State color may fill, and the ground loses its hue.

Hues stay fixed to state, but they fill halo segments, answers and soft washes behind asks. Midnight at hue 265 becomes five themes with Daylight and Eclipse as the flagships; Onest and IBM Plex Mono become Outfit and Geist Mono.

Spec 14 · Design language
("Encodings") · Iconography
The encodings become ring segments.

Currency is a segment and a sweeping mark, not a 6px pulsing dot. Importance is a four-segment gauge, not three bars. Lineage drops the square project dots. Paused becomes hollow grey instead of the attention hue, because a pause is not your move unless a bound tripped, and then it sits in Needs a call.

Spec 14 · App shell
("Sidebar foot", "Top bar")
The pulse becomes the fleet halo, and the tab goes live.

The sidebar foot's summary line becomes a ring with "3 waiting on you · 8 working · 12 idle". The spec gains a live favicon and a "(n)" title prefix, both counting what waits on you.

Spec 14 · The Intake view
(principles 1, 3 and 4)
The headline sentence becomes the dial, and Intake gets a rule for scale.

The sentence's numbers become numerals around a dial; the Now tier's pulsing dot becomes the tier's thickness on it. Past a threshold, everything after the Now tier groups by Topic, with one answer per group.

Spec 14 · App shell
(new: the quick panel)
The quick panel and the command palette are one panel.

⌘K inside the app and ⌥Space anywhere on the desktop open the same panel: what waits on you first, with its answers, then search and commands.

Spec 01 · Post-v1
Spec 14 · Post-v1 ("Desktop app")
Spec 10 · §7.4 Bound actions
Name the desktop app and the phone as one grammar.

The desktop app is already "high priority later". This design draws it (menu bar extra, quick panel, native notifications, a dock badge) and a phone (Live Activity, lock screen answers). It asks spec 10 to state that a Notification's bound actions render as native notification buttons on both.

Spec 14 · App shell
(the theme row, #186)
Five themes, not two.

The theme row's popover offers Daylight, Porcelain, Eclipse, Umber, Orchid and System (which pairs Daylight and Eclipse), previewed in Settings · Appearance. The choice stays per browser, as today.
