02

Why this one

Hercule runs on clocks. Triggers fire, triage passes every two hours, heartbeats tick, spawn bounds reset each hour, and sessions wait. Today's screens are lists sorted by recency, so time is text you have to read: "4m", "1h", "Sunday 18:10".

Tide puts it all on one axis that every screen shares, so each screen answers the same two questions: what happened while I was away, and what happens next. Waiting stops being a word and becomes a length you compare at a glance.

Other directions organize by place, source or urgency. Only Tide shows rhythm, and rhythm is what a fleet of agents has.

What it trades away. An axis costs width, so detail moves below or beside it. Some things have no good when, like a Connection's settings; there Tide is just a calm list. And a small chart asks for a second of reading before any sentence does.

04

The system

One idea runs through every part: where a thing sits in time decides its color, its shape and its place on screen.

4.1

Color doctrine: color is time

Real color, used boldly, and each hue means one thing. There is no accent color: coral is not the brand, it is Now.

Past--past

Sand. Finished work loses its color and fades into the ground.

Now--now

Coral. The Now line and anything that needs you. The one vivid warm color.

Live--live

Sea. A session or assistant working right now.

Future--future

Sea-glass, hollow or dashed: triage, heartbeats, reminders, queued input.

Failure--fail

Chart magenta, the one color that is neither time nor place.

Projects--lane-*

Each project owns a lane color. Sources stay monochrome.

Solid means it happened, or it is happening. Hollow means it will happen. Never color alone: every color travels with a mark, a shape or a word.
4.2

Five themes

Two light, three dark. The dark grounds lean green, brown and grey, never blue. Click one: the whole book and every screen below switch.

Warm paper, kelp-green ink. Pale pink-white, like the inside of a shell. Green-black water. Coral glows, sea stays calm. Warm brown-grey, for long evenings. Near-neutral grey-green, for the quietest screen.

The office

The same fleet as Check-in, drawn as a small harbour office at 09:41. It is a view, toggled with List | Office, never a second source of truth.

  • Runners are rooms, sessions sit at desks. studio-mac has 5 of 6 desks taken, build-box-1 is at its cap of 8, build-box-2 has 3 of 8.
  • The same encodings. Each pebble wears its state mark: coral waits on you, sea works, grey idles, the moon sleeps. Scarves are project lane colors.
  • Assistants sit by the windows. Their Conversations are sessions too, but not at a runner's desk: 16 at desks plus 7 Conversations make the 23.
  • Time is on the wall. The clock and the Next list show 10:00 and 11:00 in sea-glass, the same hollow marks as everywhere else.

8.2

The first five changes

  1. Swap the tokens in packages/ui.

    Replace the token file with Tide's five themes and the light and dark aliases, and self-host Sora and DM Mono. --attn stays as an alias of --now, so every existing component keeps rendering while the rest moves.

  2. One mark family in packages/ui.

    A single Mark component on the 16px grid replaces the state dots and the 12px glyphs. Solid means happened or needs you, hollow means future. The Marks legend fly-out lists the same set.

  3. One time scale, in packages/client-core and packages/ui.

    A client-core function turns records into spans in minutes from now (turns, waits, scheduled items), with its own tests. TimeAxis and NowLine in packages/ui draw them. The titlebar tideline, the wait bar and the Intake tide are its first three consumers.

  4. Rebuild the Intake route in apps/web.

    The tide and the receipt as the header, then the lead Proposal with its story strip, made-from and answer ledger (↩, A, D), then Today and When you can.

  5. The composer and thread chrome.

    The glass composer that shrinks while the transcript scrolls, the Request dock as a ledger with keys, queued input as the hollow "runs next" row, and the session tideline in the thread's title bar.

09

What this design asks the spec to change

Tide changes no domain semantics. It asks for these changes and additions to how things look and where they sit.

  • changeSpec 14 · Design languageThe recommended answer is a solid row

    The spec keeps decision affordances as quiet ghost buttons. Tide makes one answer solid, with its consequence and a key; the others stay quiet.

  • changedocs/design-language.md · ThemesFive themes and no accent color

    One light and one dark become five named themes with light and dark aliases. Coral means Now, so there is no brand accent.

  • changeSpec 14 · Iconography · design-language §MarksMarks move to a 16px grid; hollow means future

    The queued ○ is drawn in the future color, not the attention hue or faint grey, because queued input runs after Now.

  • changeSpec 14 · The composerThe composer narrows while you scroll

    From 720 to 580 with max-width and margin, not a transform, so text stays crisp. The glass thins from 74% to 52%; the blur is 10px.

  • additionSpec 14 · App shellA tideline in the titlebar; on web, a rail and a docked Now column

    At 1280 and wider, the web app shows what waits elsewhere, this hour in the workspace, and what comes next.

  • additionSpec 14 · The Intake view · Spec 10 §8Intake leads with the tide and a receipt

    The tide since the last check and one square per event come before the tiers. The tiers and their order are unchanged.

  • additionSpec 10 §2.4 · The answers on a proposalBatch answers at swarm scale

    Same-shaped Proposals group, and one answer applies to each Proposal in the group. Each still records its own answer.

  • additionSpec 14 · The check-in viewAnswer N in a row

    Steps through what waits on you, oldest first, one decision per screen, until none are left.

  • additionSpec 14 · Settings > AppearanceFollow macOS, shown as a sun strip

    A 24-hour strip shows which theme will show when: Sand by day, Kelp by night.

  • additionSpec 14 · Packages and desktop-shell readinessMenu bar extra and quick panel

    The desktop app answers decisions outside its window. The office counts assistant Conversations apart from runner desks (23 = 16 + 7).
